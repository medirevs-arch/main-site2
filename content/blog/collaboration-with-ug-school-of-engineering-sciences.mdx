Medirevs Labs exists because software can move a lab result across the country
but it cannot produce the result. Somewhere at the start of that chain, a
physical instrument has to exist, work, and be affordable enough for the
facility to own one.

That work is no longer ours alone.

> Grateful for this partnership with the School of Engineering Sciences. Proud
> of what the student team is building, and we're excited to keep supporting
> their work in microscopy.

## The visit

In July our team paid a courtesy call on the Dean of the School of Engineering
Sciences to deliver supplies to the student team. The School described the
visit this way:

> Winner Ahadzivia and his team from Medirevs paid a courtesy call on the Dean
> of the School of Engineering Sciences today to deliver supplies to the
> student team collaborating with the company on the design and construction
> of low-cost microscopes. The update was positive: the students are making
> steady progress on the project.

## Why it matters to us

A teaching microscope that a department can actually afford to own is not a
research curiosity. It is the difference between a laboratory course that
happens and one that does not, and between a diagnosis made locally and a
sample that has to travel.

Getting there means optics, mechanical design, and a lot of testing against
reality rather than against drawings — which is exactly the kind of work a
student engineering team should be doing, and exactly the kind of instrument
that should be designed where it will be used.

We will keep publishing what we learn, either way.
