Paper looks free. It has no licence fee, no server, no training budget, and no
vendor to call when it breaks. That appearance is the reason it survives, and it
is why the case for digital records so often gets made badly, as a pitch about
being modern rather than an honest account of what paper costs.

The cost is real. It is simply paid by people rather than by the finance office.

## Where the cost actually falls

It falls on the patient who repeats their history at every visit, because the
folder is in a different building, or in a different clinic entirely, or in the
same room but unfindable.

It falls on the clinician who makes a decision with less information than exists,
because retrieving that information would take longer than the consultation.

It falls on the pharmacist reading handwriting, and on the laboratory
transcribing a request by hand, and on the facility that cannot answer a simple
question about its own operations without someone counting folders.

None of that appears as a line item. All of it is expenditure.

> A record you cannot find is not a record. It is a receipt for work someone
> already paid for once.

## Why "just digitise it" is the wrong instruction

The failure mode of health IT is a system that assumes conditions it does not
have. It assumes a stable connection. It assumes a desktop computer. It assumes
that the facility has someone whose job is to maintain software. It assumes that
every party in an episode of care uses the same system.

When any of those assumptions breaks, and in most clinics several break at once,
the digital system becomes a second system running next to paper, and now the
staff keep both going. That is worse than where they started, and it is the
reason so many deployments quietly revert.

So the requirement is not digitisation. The requirement is a record that survives
the conditions of the place it is used in:

- It works when the connection does not, and reconciles when the connection
  returns.
- It runs on the device people already have.
- It moves between the parties who touch one episode of care.
- It is faster than the paper it replaces on the very first day, not after a
  three-month adjustment period.

That last one is the hard constraint. A system that is slower than a folder will
lose to the folder, and it should.

## What we are building toward

Medirevs EHR is being developed against these constraints rather than around
them. Offline-first is not a feature on a list; it is the assumption the system
is built on. The same goes for interoperability. A record that cannot move
between a clinic, a pharmacy and a lab has solved storage and left the real
problem alone.

We will write more about this as the work develops, including the parts that turn
out to be harder than expected.
