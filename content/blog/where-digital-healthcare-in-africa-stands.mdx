> Millions of people in Africa still struggle to access basic healthcare. Not
> because they don't want it, but because the system cannot reach them.

That distinction matters more than it might sound. Demand for care is not the
constraint. Reach is. A person who wants to see a clinician and cannot is a
logistics failure, not a behavioural one — and logistics failures are the kind
of problem that infrastructure can actually address.

Which is where digital health has been oversold and under-delivered in equal
measure. The tools work in a demo with full signal. The corridor has one bar,
a shared handset, and a queue.

So the useful question is not whether healthcare in Africa is going digital.
It is which parts of the system a connection can genuinely carry, what has to
keep working when that connection drops, and who is left out by each decision
along the way.

That is the ground we are trying to build on.
