Software can move a lab result to a clinician in a different city. It cannot
produce the result. Somewhere at the start of that chain, a physical instrument
has to exist, work, and be affordable enough for the facility to own one.

That is the gap Medirevs Labs exists to work in.

## The limit of a purely digital approach

Everything Medirevs has built so far assumes that a diagnosis can be made and
then needs to travel. Connect the parties, hold the record, close the loop.

But a connected system inherits the limits of what it connects. If a facility
does not have working diagnostic equipment, the most elegant referral pathway in
the world routes a patient toward an absence.

Microscopy is one of the clearest examples. It sits underneath a lot of routine
lab work, and the equipment is expensive to buy, expensive to maintain, and hard
to fix once it breaks.

## The first project

Medirevs Labs is developing a low cost microscope with a student team from the
University of Ghana School of Engineering Sciences, who are working on the design
and construction of low cost microscopes.

We are deliberately not publishing specifications yet. No magnification figures,
no target unit cost, no timeline. Those numbers matter too much to be estimated
in public before the work supports them, and a project page full of confident
placeholders is how good engineering gets turned into marketing.

What we can describe is the shape of the problem:

- **Cost is the design constraint,** not an outcome to optimise afterwards.
- **Serviceability matters as much as performance.** An instrument that cannot be
  repaired locally has a short working life regardless of how well it performs on
  day one.
- **The deployment environment is the specification.** Heat, dust, power
  interruptions and non-specialist operators are the conditions, not edge cases.
